# 虚ろな料理店 (*Utsuro na Ryōriten*) — Chapter 1: The Feast of Hollow Wood

The woodcutters of the foothills called it a phantom; the village elders called it an illusion woven by ancient cedar roots.

According to rumor, far past the moss-crusted boundary stones where footpaths dissolved into damp fern, stood a grand Western-style restaurant. At twilight, pale white vapor and fine gray ash drifted lazily from its leaning brick chimneys, carrying the unmistakable scent of toasted butter, caramelized mountain sugar, and spiced chestnut paste. Through its cloudy, stained-glass windows, wanderers swore they could hear the rise and swell of a full orchestra—strings shivering with nervous delight, a flute darting like an autumn swallow, and a muted brass heartbeat keeping steady time.

Yet hunters who peered through the brass keyhole found only silence. Inside, the grand hall was completely gutted: fallen ceiling beams, rotting floorboards, and thick sheets of gray cobwebs shuddering in the cold drafts. No hearth, no tables, no diners. A phantom feast cooked by empty air.

The elders warned every child never to step toward the smell of sweet smoke.

***

The little girl had not been searching for sweet smoke. She had been chasing a shimmering green jewel-beetle, clutching a wide, ribbed taro leaf over her head like an umbrella to keep the thick mountain dew from dripping into her hair.

Her white canvas shoes were damp and stained dark green around the rubber edges. *Pacha, pacha.* Each step squelched into the soft black loam. Around her, the forest stood immensely tall, straight, and ancient, draped in bearded moss that swallowed every rustle of the wind. 

*Fuuu... rururu...*

The faint, clear trill of a wooden flute rippled through the mist.

The girl halted, tilting her taro leaf. 

*Zazaa—* She parted a heavy curtain of wild purple wisteria and emerged into an expansive clearing. 

The rumors had painted a rotting shack, but the building before her was breathtakingly grandiose, like an antique clockwork palace left behind by forgotten nobility. It rose three stories high, crowned with steep mansard roofs, blackened gables, and polished copper weathervanes shaped like bounding hares. Five stout brick chimneys huffed rhythmically into the twilight sky: *Puff-puff, puff-puff.* The air tasted of warm honey and roasted barley.

Golden candlelight spilled from every tall arched window, casting a warm amber glow over the stone staircase. From inside came the unmistakable melody of a lively waltz: *Zun-cha-cha, zun-cha-cha.*

The girl blinked, tucked her leaf tightly under her arm, and climbed the steps. *Katan, katan.*

The heavy front door was adorned with a brass knocker cast in the shape of a sleeping fox. Reaching up with both hands, she gave it a firm push. *Giiii—*

The heavy door glided open, and the cold mountain air vanished behind her.

The waltz did not stop; it grew fuller, wrapping around her shoulders like a heated blanket. And the grand hall was not empty at all.

Standing behind a carved mahogany reception desk was a slender Japanese crane dressed in an immaculate, starch-stiff white apron. It peered down at her with gentle, bead-black eyes, adjusted a small pair of wire-rimmed spectacles resting on its long bill, and bowed low. *Katakata, koto.* Its beak clicked twice in polite, formal greeting.

The crane extended a snowy wing, delicately slid the damp taro leaf from the girl’s grasp, and placed it with theatrical care into an umbrella stand made of beaten silver.

*Chirin, chirin.*

A tiny silver bell tinkled, and the dining room came alive. 

The hall was vast and warm. Chandeliers woven from intertwined honeysuckle vines and trapped, luminous green fireflies swayed gently beneath vaulted cedar ceilings. In the far alcove, an orchestra was in full swing: four plump tanuki in tiny, velvet-tailed tuxedo coats bowed vigorously upon gourd-carved violins (*giko-giko, giko-giko*), while a fat badger puffed its cheeks out like two white dumplings to blow into a polished wooden bassoon (*booo, booo-bop*). 

None of them spoke a word. No human syllables broke the rhythm. Yet every pair of eyes in the hall turned toward the little girl with overwhelming, affectionate delight.

*Tatatata!*

A young red fox with three fluffy, ember-tipped tails trotted across the polished cedar planks, balancing a round brass tray neatly upon its wet nose. Stopping before the girl, the fox cocked its head sideways. *Kyuu?*

With a playful wag of its bushy tails, it used its soft hindquarters to gently nudge a heavy, velvet-cushioned armchair right behind her knees. *Sutan.* The girl fell back into the plush seat with a soft bounce.

Before she could even tuck her canvas shoes under the rungs, the kitchen swing-doors burst open: *Batan, batan!*

A troop of white field mice wearing miniature tall chef toques scurried out, pushing a two-tiered trolley with squeaking wheels. *Koro-koro-koro.* They hopped onto the mahogany tabletop with nimble paws, their whiskers twitching in unison. 

With frantic, doting care, they set down a feast.

*Don!* A deep porcelain bowl filled with plump mountain berries, stewed until glossy and dusted with fine crystal sugar.

*Koto.* A steaming lacquered dish holding three golden sweet-potato cakes, sizzling faintly with honey-butter glaze: *Juuu...*

*Poko!* A wooden cup overflowing with hot roasted barley tea that smelled of sun-dried grain.

The little girl took a silver spoon. *Mogu, mogu.* 

The berry sauce burst sweet and tart across her tongue. Her eyes widened. *Oishii.*

The moment she swallowed, the creatures seemed to lose their minds with joy. The fox let out a happy yap—*yan-yan!*—and spun in three rapid circles. The crane flapped its wings in silent applause (*basa, basa*). And behind the kitchen doors, pans clattered with renewed, frantic urgency: *Kashan! Goton!*

They didn't just want to feed her; they wanted to stuff her.

*Suu—* A masked racoon dog waddled over carrying a wooden trencher piled high with fluffy white milk buns, soft as rabbit fur (*fuka-fuka*). Before she could finish her first sweet-potato cake, a pair of barn owls in tweed waistcoats fluttered down from the chandeliers, gingerly placing a dish of crystallized persimmons right beside her elbow: *Katsu.*

The little mice hovered over her fingers, gently nudging the spoon back toward the bowls whenever she paused to breathe. *Chichi! Chichi!* They pointed their tiny pink paws at the buns, then at the persimmons, gazing up at her with giant, glossy black eyes full of pleading adoration. 

She took another bite. *Paku, mogu, mogu.*

Then a milk bun. *Ham-ham.* The dough melted like sweetened cream.

The music grew louder, faster, and more celebratory. *Zun-cha-cha, ran-ran-ran!* The tanuki violinists stomped their padded paws to the beat (*ton, ton, ton*), their eyes crinkling shut in pure ecstasy as they watched the human child eat.

Whenever the girl’s chin dipped, another dish appeared out of nowhere. A steaming bowl of candied chestnuts steeped in sweet plum nectar (*toro-toro*). A plate of crisp lotus chips dusted with ginger sugar (*paki-paki*). Every time she reached for tea to wash it down, the crane was already leaning over her shoulder with an earthenware kettle, refilling her cup with a graceful, uninterrupted stream: *Chorochoro...*

"Um... I'm full," the girl whispered softly, patting her stomach.

The fox tilted its head, whine-trilling softly—*kyuuu-u?*—and nudged a warm, golden persimmon slice directly against her knuckles. The mice clasped their tiny paws together under their chins, quivering with such puppy-eyed anticipation that she couldn't say no.

*Gokun.* She swallowed another chestnut.

Her small belly grew round, tight, and heavy, swelling out against the waistband of her navy cotton shorts like a ripe melon. She leaned back against the plush velvet, her eyelids drooping with overwhelming, drowsy warmth. Every breath she took felt stuffed with cinnamon, honey, and roasted grain. 

*Haaah...* A tiny, contented sigh escaped her lips.

Instantly, the frantic clatter ceased.

The waltz smoothed out into a slow, gentle lullaby. The badger lowered its bassoon with a soft *shu-u*. The fox stepped onto the chair, carefully tucked a soft linen napkin under the girl’s chin, and licked a stray crumb of sugar from the corner of her cheek: *Pero.*

The crane glided forward on silent talons, retrieved her damp taro leaf from the silver stand, and held it out with both wings.

The girl wobbled to her feet. *Yorori.* Her stomach was so round and heavy that she had to brace both hands across her belly just to maintain balance. *Pote, pote.* She waddled toward the entrance like a well-fed duckling, burping a tiny bubble of sweet honey air: *Geppu.*

The crane bowed until its beak touched the cedar planks. The fox wagged all three tails in a lazy, satisfied swirl. The mice lined up along the skirting board, waving their chef hats: *Buri-buri.*

*Giiii— katan.*

The heavy door clicked shut behind her.

The cool forest breeze hit her flushed cheeks. The girl turned around on the top step, blinking through the evening dusk.

Behind her, there was no brass knocker. No stained-glass waltz. No golden light.

Only a moss-eaten, rotted wooden gate standing over collapsed timbers and hollow floorboards, dead silent except for the chirping of evening crickets: *Kiri-kiri-kiri...*

She looked down at her hands. 

In her right hand, she still held her wide taro leaf. Tied to its stem by a strand of dry wild grass was a small parcel folded from a clean bamboo sheath—stuffed to bursting with warm candied chestnuts.

And inside her, resting heavy, tight, and warm, her full belly gurgled in deep, sleepy contentment: *Goro-goro...*
