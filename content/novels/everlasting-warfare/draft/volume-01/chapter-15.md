The world was nothing but a void of white and screaming wind.

I walked without a path, without a destination, stepping blindly across the endless, cracked expanse of Antarctic ice. Every step sank into the frozen crust with a dull, hollow crunch. I didn't know how long I had been walking, nor did I care where the next step would take me. Time had lost its meaning. The horizon was swallowed by a blinding blizzard that swept across the barren white desert, howling like a dying beast.

On my shoulder, the black crow shifted its weight. Its sharp, dark talons dug tightly into the fabric of my shoulder to keep from being torn away by the gale. It did not caw. It did not make a sound. It simply clung to me, a silent watcher perched upon a ghost.

My gray mage cloak fluttered violently in the storm, its frayed layers whipping around my legs. The outer layer was shattered and shredded—haggard strips of heavy wool torn apart by violent force. Across the front, a wide, dark stain of frozen blood stiffened the fabric into a jagged crust. It wasn't my blood. Or perhaps some of it was. I could no longer remember whose blood had painted my chest before the cold turned it into dark, rigid ice.

The brim of my mage hat—far too big for my head—tilted low over my brow under the weight of the wind. It shadowed my face, keeping the stinging ice crystals from striking my skin, though it mattered little. 

My eyes were bleak. Hollowed out. Deprived of spark or color, reflecting only the vast, empty nightmare of the storm.

I clutched my staff. It was a simple, unadorned piece of plain wood—no glowing crystals, no golden bands, no arcane engravings etched into its bark. Just a rough, bare branch smoothed by years of handling. 

A sudden violent gust of wind slammed against my side, threatening to knock me off my feet. My knees buckled slightly. I drove the tip of the wooden staff down, tucking it firmly into the hard ice ground, leaning my entire weight against the shaft to hold myself upright against the tempest. 

The frost clung to my eyelashes. The blizzard raged around me with violent, sub-zero cruelty, yet the biting cold could not bother me. The freezing air, the sub-zero gale that should have turned blood to glass—it felt like nothing. The numbness inside my chest was far deeper than any winter the world could forge. 

I stared out into the whirling sea of white, my chest heaving with shallow, ragged breaths. 

"Those monsters... everyone... is gone..." I whispered into the howling storm.

My voice was exhausted, barely a rasp, swallowed instantly by the roaring wind. 

The silence that followed inside my head was deafening. The grand halls, the quiet dorm room, the patient voice of my professor, the frantic scramble of spell formulas—all of it had been erased. Scorched into ash, broken into dust, and buried beneath a mountain of corpses. There was no academy left to return to. No lectures to skip. No quiet sanctuary. 

Ahead, through the curtain of blinding frost, the dark, jagged mouth of a natural ice cave emerged from the side of a massive glacier ridge. 

I trudged toward it, dragging my staff through the snow until I passed beneath the overhanging stone and ice. As I stepped deeper into the cavern, the deafening roar of the blizzard receded into a muffled, distant moan. The air inside was still, heavy with darkness and the stale scent of ancient earth.

I sank to my knees upon the frozen floor, letting out a long, weary sigh. 

Reaching down with my free hand, I pulled a small, sharp utility blade from my belt and sliced a ragged square of wool from the bottom hem of my shattered gray cloak. The heavy fabric came away with a dull tear. 

Using the tip of the blade, I carved a precise geometric magic circle directly into the hard ice floor—a familiar combustion matrix I had mapped out hundreds of times in my notebook back at the academy. I placed the cut scrap of gray wool square in the center of the carved geometric runes.

Then, lifting my wooden staff, I tapped its unadorned tip against the center of the circle.

*Click.*

A faint spark of blue mana flashed at the point of contact, tracing the etched lines in an instant. The conditional trigger activated, and a warm, steady amber flame erupted from the scrap of wool, illuminating the dark cave walls in flickering light and casting long, dancing shadows behind us.

The crow fluttered off my shoulder and hopped down onto the dry stone near the warmth of the fire. 

"Ka! Ka!" it rasped softly, shaking the frost from its dark obsidian feathers.

I looked down at the bird, a faint, fleeting ghost of a shadow touching my bleak eyes. 

"Kaka..." I murmured quietly. 

I had named him Kaka years ago, simply because that was the incessant, noisy sound he made—*Ka! Ka!*—whenever he perched on my dormitory windowsill, demanding crumbs while I stayed up until dawn sketching spell matrices. He was loud, impatient, and stubbornly persistent. In a world where everyone else questioned my research or forced me into rigid doctrines, Kaka had stayed by my side. He was my only true friend.

Kaka tilted his head, letting out another low *"Ka!"* before stretching one wing toward the fire's heat.

I leaned back against the cave wall, resting my head against the cold stone, and closed my eyes to channel my mana.

*Eagle Eye Magic.*

My perception detached from my physical body, surging upward through the ceiling of the cavern and piercing straight into the storm above. My sensory view expanded exponentially, rising high into the grey sky like a bird soaring above the clouds. 

Through the roaring veil of white, my mind traced the contours of the frozen continent. Miles away to the north, where the glacial plains plunged violently into the churning ocean, I located it—a jagged, dark shoreline battered by icy waves and black coastal rocks. 

Satisfied with the coordinate, I severed the sensory link. 

My vision snapped back to the dim cave and the flickering amber fire. I opened my eyes slowly, the bleakness returning to my gaze as the weight of my exhaustion settled back over my shoulders.

Reaching deep into the inner pocket of my tunic, my fingers brushed against a small, hardened piece of ration bread—one of the last scraps left from my supplies. 

I pulled out the stale bread, broke it carefully in half, and extended one piece toward the fire. 

Kaka hopped closer, took his half gently from my fingers with his sharp beak, and began pecking at it greedily. I brought my own half to my lips, chewing slowly on the dry crust, watching the quiet flames consume the scrap of cloak in the dark, frozen silence.

---

Most mages spent their lives worrying about mana. They measured their reserves with paranoid precision, dreading the onset of arcane exhaustion or the sudden depletion of their internal stores. 

I never concerned myself with mana. 

To me, mana was not a precious liquid to be hoarded in a small internal vessel. It was an infinite ocean flowing through the planet’s leylines, pulsing through the atmosphere, the ice, and the air. Because of my Passive Magic matrices, I could draw ambient mana directly from the world around me whenever I wanted, seamlessly refilling my channel without effort or incantation. 

Finishing the last bite of bread, I stood up from the cave floor. The small fire turned into a dying ember, fading into cold ash behind me. 

I held my simple wooden staff loosely in my right hand and primed a Levitation Magic formula. 

*Shift.*

Gravity released its grip on my body. My boots lifted off the icy stone floor, hovering effortlessly a foot above the ground. Friction vanished, and the heavy burden of physical weight dissolved into smooth, weightless suspension.

Instead of taking flight on his own dark wings, Kaka fluttered up from the stone and landed directly back onto my shoulder, digging his claws into the frayed wool of my gray cloak to settle into a comfortable roost.

I tilted my head sideways, gazing at him with bleak, unblinking eyes beneath the oversized brim of my hat.

"Aren't you a bird?" I muttered dryly.

Kaka didn't bother opening his eyes. He simply tucked his beak beneath his feathers and let out a lazy, muffled rasp.

*"Ka."*

A tiny, dry exhale escaped my lips. Leaning forward into the air, I floated out from the dark ice cave and launched myself into the roaring tempest. 

The Levitation Magic propelled us smoothly across the sky, gliding through the blinding wall of snow and wind. Boundless ambient mana surged into my spell grid, sustaining the flight without a hint of strain. Hovering high above the treacherous glacial crevasses and jagged ice spikes, we cut a quiet, weightless path through the blizzard, heading straight toward the distant shoreline.

---

The howling storm abruptly dropped away as we crossed the coastal ridge. 

High above the shore, the thick blizzard clouds parted, leaving a quiet, clear expanse of grey sky. Below us, black coastal rocks stood as jagged sentinels against the dark, freezing waters of the ocean, where gentle waves rolled against the ice cliffs.

I canceled the Levitation Magic, allowing my boots to touch down softly upon a high, rocky outcrop overlooking the sea. 

I sat down near the edge of the cliff, drawing my knees slightly toward my chest and resting the plain wooden staff across my lap. The silence of the high ground settled over me, vast and hollow. 

I sat there, staring blankly ahead, completely stripped of purpose. For the first time in my life, I didn't know what to do anymore. There were no dorm room doors to lock, no explosive spell tests to refine, no professor to visit, and no target for my research. The entire world felt like a vast, empty graveyard.

I glanced sideways at Kaka. The black crow was no help at all—he simply preened his wing feathers in the still air, completely unbothered by the aimless void stretching out before us.

With a quiet breath, I raised my gaze toward the far ocean horizon.

My eyes widened slightly. The bleakness in my stare flickered as my breath caught in my throat.

There, far out across the open sea, rising impossibly from the water and piercing through the upper layers of the sky, stood a colossal, glowing World Tree. Its colossal trunk burned with ancient emerald and golden light, its massive branches stretching across the heavens like the pillars of creation itself.

I stared at the divine titan towering over the world, the memories of long nights studying ancient texts flooding back to me in a rush.

"That tree... just like in the book of Merlin," I whispered softly to the empty sky.

A long, quiet pause followed. I pulled the brim of my oversized hat up slightly, watching the radiant light of the distant tree shimmer against the dark ocean waves.

"So it's not a myth."

Then, faintly—piercing through the calm lap of the coastal waves—a sound echoed from far along the lower shore. 

It was a direction I hadn't looked toward before. 

A voice. Small, weak, and frantic, carrying across the freezing sea. Someone was shouting for help, drowning in the icy black waters near a shipwrecked vessel.

My heart jolted with sudden surprise. 

Before I could even process the sound or process a single thought, a suffocating, terrifying pressure slammed into my mind like a falling iron gate.

My body locked up.

Every muscle in my torso snapped rigid. My right hand clamped around my wooden staff with a crushing force that nearly splintered the wood. My legs forced themselves upward, standing my body straight upon the rocky ledge like a helpless marionette pulled by unseen, invisible strings.

Horror—raw, terrifying, absolute horror—flooded my veins. 

My eyes dilated wide in pure panic as I looked down at my own legs moving without my consent. My arms raised the staff against my will, channeling an immense, violent surge of raw mana through my palms.

*What is happening to me?!* 

I screamed inside my own head, fighting with every ounce of my will to force my fingers to loosen, to force my knees to bend, to regain control of a single inch of my own flesh. But my body completely ignored me. I was a helpless bystander locked inside a hijacked shell.

I frantically searched through the archives of my memory, tearing through every passive spell matrix, every geometric formula, every conditional trigger I had ever mapped or tested in my dormitory room. *Did I set a trigger for this?! Is this a dormant passive spell malfunctioning?!*

*No.* 

I had never forged a spell like this. I had never programmed a matrix that overrode my own consciousness, nor had I ever created a trigger to drag my body to play hero for strangers in the sea. This wasn't my formula. This wasn't my spell.

The crushing pressure around me exploded into a roaring vortex. Ambient mana from the leylines folded around the cliff, distorting light, space, and gravity in a terrifying arcane crunch.

Kaka let out a panicked, shrieking *"KA!"*, fluttering wildly off my shoulder as the violent spatial distortion threatened to tear his feathers apart.

My staff thrust forward into the air, locking into a high-tier mass teleportation matrix. 

Space collapsed inward with a deafening, sickening tear. 

In a flash of blinding, horrifying light, I vanished from the cliff—leaving Kaka behind, fluttering alone above the empty stone.

---

*SPLASH!*

Ice-cold seawater slammed into my face with violent, crushing force. 

Freezing ocean brine surged into my nose and throat. I gagged, choking as the dark, tempestuous swells swallowed me whole. Towering waves crashed over my head, threatening to drag me into the fathomless depths of the freezing abyss.

Yet my limbs refused to flail. My hijacked body remained unnaturally rigid, thrusting upward through the churning surf like an unyielding spear. 

Wiping the sea spray from my eyes, I gasped for air and looked out across the water. 

There he was—the man who had been screaming. He was clinging frantically to a jagged piece of floating wooden debris, his face pale with hypothermia, his lungs raw from shouting across the sea. Around him lay a scene of utter, apocalyptic carnage. A colossal warship had been snapped completely in half, its iron hull torn like thin paper. Dozens of survivors were flailing in the freezing swells, screaming in desperation as they clung to barrels and floating wreckage.

And in the dark, storm-torn sky above them...

*Swarm.*

Hundreds of Metallic-Bodied Monsters circled like giant, mechanical vultures. Their polished steel frames gleamed under the grey clouds as their razor-sharp limbs and glowing red ocular lenses rained horror down upon the drowning victims. It was a terrifying nightmare beyond my comprehension—a metallic horde of unnatural entities I had never encountered in any scroll or treatise.

Driven by the invisible, relentless force puppeteering my limbs, my right arm thrust the simple wooden staff forward into the ocean.

*BOOM!*

A colossal shockwave exploded across the sea. The ocean split in half with a deafening roar! 

Colossal walls of dark, freezing water were blasted violently aside, creating a massive, dry canyon in the middle of the sea. Towering walls of ocean hovered suspended in mid-air, held back by a dome of pure, crushing pressure.

Without a second's delay, my staff channeled every remaining drop of stored mana in one final, catastrophic surge. The strain was immense—I felt the internal mana core of my staff drain completely hollow, completely empty.

The high-tier mass spatial trigger fired.

Space collapsed inward with a blinding, tearing flash. 

*FLASH!*

The drowning survivors, the man who had been shouting, and I were instantly warped out of the ocean canyon.

We tumbled hard onto the frozen surface of the high coastal cliff—the exact spot where Kaka was waiting. The survivors collapsed onto the snow, coughing up seawater, gasping in complete shock.

The moment the teleportation spell completed, the invisible puppet strings severed.

The extreme magical strain took its toll. My legs buckled instantly under the sudden, staggering weight of total physical and arcane exhaustion. I fell to my knees in complete physical collapse, my vision spinning in dizzying darkness as the wooden staff slipped from my numb fingers.

Kaka fluttered down onto the icy rock, letting out a frantic, worried *"KA!"* as he hopped toward me.

Trembling in terror with wide, frightened eyes, I looked up at the shivering man and his surviving crew lying in the snow—completely horrified by what my own body had just done.

Before I could speak, before I could utter a single word, the 100% total depletion of my staff's mana triggered an emergency fail-safe in my passive layer.

An automated defense matrix—programmed to protect my life upon total mana exhaustion—instantly activated.

A blue spark of light engulfed my collapsing form. 

Before anyone could touch me, before Kaka could land on my shoulder, I vanished into thin air a second time—leaving the shivering survivors and Kaka behind on the snowy cliff.
