The golden step-pyramid loomed above us like a monument forgotten by time, its massive tiering cutting through the howling Antarctic gale. Now that the optical cloak had dissolved, the radiant golden metal seemed to absorb and reflect whatever faint ambient light filtered through the snowstorm, casting a warm, shimmering glow over the frozen waste.

"Move up!" I commanded, shielding my eyes against the wind as I gestured toward the base of the structure. "Don't linger out on the snow! The heat conduits along the stairways will cut the chill!"

The survivors didn't need to be told twice. Exhausted, frostbitten, and trembling violently from the sub-zero trek across the glacier, the fifty-two refugees scrambled toward the perimeter of the colossal ziggurat.

The architecture was unmistakable—a deliberate homage to ancient Etemenanki, the legendary foundation of heaven and earth. Rather than a single grand staircase cutting directly up the front, the base of the ziggurat featured massive lateral side staircases running parallel along the outer walls. High above, intermediate cross-stairways crisscrossed back inward, intersecting at designated landings before converging toward the high central core.

Azure moved beside me, her mechanical suit humming softly as she effortlessly pulled Liezt’s heavy Aegis crystal pod behind her. The blue light from the stasis lattice reflected off the polished golden steps as we mounted the first lateral ramp.

"The thermal radiance is active," Azure noted, her quiet voice crisp despite the lingering storm. "Surface temperature along the golden steps is calibrated to twelve degrees Celsius. Hypothermia risk among the civilian contingent is decreasing."

As soon as our boots hit the golden stone of the side staircase, the razor-sharp bite of the Antarctic wind receded. Warmth radiated upward through the soles of our boots, fed by internal heat exchangers built deep within the ziggurat’s superstructure.

I led the column up the side ramp, keeping a steady pace as we climbed toward the first cross-landing. Erika marched right at my shoulder, her heavy leather boots clicking rhythmically against the warm metal. Kaka remained securely perched on her collar, his obsidian feathers fluffed up as he let out a comfortable *"Ka..."*, clearly enjoying the sudden influx of warmth.

"This design..." Erika murmured, her sharp eyes scanning the intricate geometric patterns etched into the golden walls beside us as we reached the first cross-staircase and turned inward toward the center. "It isn't just aesthetic, is it?"

"No," I replied, keeping my eyes fixed on the central platform ahead. "Etemenanki structural geometry was engineered to distribute weight and energy flux evenly across seismic fault lines. It also forces any approaching force to bottleneck along the intersecting cross-stairways, making the central core virtually impregnable from the ground."

As we traversed the cross-stairway toward the center, the sheer scale of the facility became apparent. Beneath the golden shell lay three centuries of Twilight engineering.

"If the internal seals held up during the spatial distortion," I continued, speaking over the low hum of the structure's awakening power grid, "Zyfel should still be operational inside. And... if they survived, Rosette and Sakura White might be down there too."

Erika glanced at me, her expression tightening with a sharp, sarcastic edge at the mention of those two names. She adjusted the collar of her coat, letting out a heavy breath that misted in the air before dissipating against the warm ambient heat.

"Of course they're down there," Erika said dryly. "You sent Rosette and Sakura White to infiltrate The Crows as your little Twilight spies, remember? Did you really think I wouldn't notice? Long before this world ever went under maintenance, I caught on to your scheme—so I shipped both of your precious spies straight to Antarctica, right into the middle of my army's operational zone."

I glanced at her sideways as we kept climbing. "Your army?"

"Seven hundred Crows," Erika answered, her eyes narrowing as she looked up toward the central core platform. "Seven hundred fully armed personnel under my command. I dispatched them here on a long-term mission long before the maintenance hit. Sakura and Rosette have been stuck with them ever since. If your automated systems didn't wipe them out on sight, there should be a full garrison waiting down there."

"Seven hundred..." I murmured, processing the number. Seven hundred battle-tested fighters tucked away inside the subterranean tiers would drastically alter our tactical standing. "Assuming the facility's life support remained intact during the blackout, they would have had access to our auxiliary rations and atmospheric scrubbers."

We reached the top of the converging cross-staircase, stepping out onto the vast, circular plaza at the very center of the ziggurat's main tier. 

The floor beneath us was composed of concentric rings of dark, polished alloy inlaid with glowing blue Twilight circuitry. High walls of gold surrounded the perimeter, shielding the plaza entirely from the freezing Antarctic gusts outside.

Dr. Griffin, Belle, and J.J brought up the rear of the column, guiding the weary refugees into the center of the ring. Many of the Crows collapsed onto the warm metallic floor with audible sighs of relief, massaging their frozen limbs and soaking in the heat.

Erika walked to the edge of the circular plaza, looking for a door or an entryway leading inside the upper structure, but found only seamless golden walls.

"Alright, Zeeran," Erika said, turning back to me with her arms crossed. "We're at the center. Where's the door? I don't see an entrance."

I walked to the exact midpoint of the circular platform, stopping directly over a prominent silver Twilight crest embedded in the floor.

"You're standing on it," I said smoothly.

Erika blinked. "What?"

"The central plaza isn't a courtyard, Erika," I explained, placing my heel firmly on the center of the crest and tapping it twice. "It's an elevator."

*CLACK-CLACK.*

A deep, reverberating pulse vibrated through the floor. The concentric alloy rings beneath our feet unlocked with a heavy pneumatic hiss, and the glowing blue conduits flared with intense radiance. 

*WHIRRRRRRR—!*

"This platform is a heavy-duty industrial lift," I said as the mechanical hum deepened. "It descends directly down the central shaft of the Ziggurat, penetrating two kilometers of solid granite bedrock into the primary subterranean research complex."

The survivors stirred in surprise as the platform shuddered, preparing to lower into the earth. However, as Erika looked at the size of the central lift ring and then back at the fifty-two refugees, her tactical instinct immediately kicked in.

She stepped forward, raising a hand to stall the automated sequence.

"Wait," Erika called out sharply.

I looked at her, raising an eyebrow. "Is there a problem?"

"Look at the lift mechanics, Zeeran," Erika said, pointing to the structural clearance edge along the platform's perimeter. "The lift can handle the weight, but if seven hundred of my Crows are down there—or if the lower levels are compromised—dropping fifty-two exhausted, half-frozen civilians down a two-kilometer shaft all at once is a tactical nightmare. If something goes wrong at the bottom, we'll be trapped in a bottleneck with no cover."

I considered her point. She was right. We had no idea what condition the lower levels were in, nor whether Zyfel or the seven hundred Crows were hostile, compromised, or under siege.

"What do you propose?" I asked.

"We split the group into half," Erika decided firmly, her command voice taking over. "First group goes down with you and Azure to clear the landing zone, verify the environment, and establish contact with whoever is down there. Second group stays up here on the heated upper tier until the lift returns with an all-clear signal."

She looked around at the weary crowd, her eyes settling on Dr. Griffin and Belle.

"Erika," I said, eyeing her closely. "Who stays behind to command the second group?"

Erika offered a faint, resolute smirk, placing a hand on her hip while Kaka shifted on her shoulder.

"I stay for the second group," Erika replied without hesitation. "I'm not leaving these people unguarded on an open platform until I know the basement is secure. Take Azure, take Liezt's pod, and take ten of our best Ravens. Go down first, Zeeran. Clear the way."
