The world tore itself apart in a blinding flash of spatial white, and then slammed into my chest with the crushing weight of frozen earth.

*THUD!*

I tumbled violently across hard, ice-crusted snow, skinning my palms and knees against jagged frost. Freezing seawater sprayed from my mouth as I gasped for air, my lungs burning violently from the ocean brine I had swallowed seconds earlier. The sound of roaring waves and the mechanical screech of the metallic horde vanished in an instant, replaced by the howling moan of an Antarctic gale sweeping across high ground.

I pushed myself up onto all fours, shivering uncontrollably, my clothes drenched in sub-zero brine that threatened to freeze solid against my skin in seconds. 

"What... what just happened?" a panicked voice screamed nearby.

"Where are we?! Where is the ship?!"

Around me, dozens of survivors from the Ark were tumbling onto the snow—coughing, screaming, and flailing in disoriented horror. Seconds ago, the Ark's hull had snapped under the relentless assault of hundreds of Metallic-Bodied Monsters, dragging us all into the black ocean abyss. Now, the ocean was gone.

Directly in front of me, sitting on her knees in the heavy drift, was the girl who had done the impossible. 

She wore an oversized, frayed gray mage hat and a haggard cloak stained with frozen blood. A simple wooden staff lay in the snow beside her numb, trembling fingers. Her eyes were wide with sheer, unadulterated terror—as if she had no idea how she had split the ocean, or why her body had moved on its own to pull fifty drowning souls out of the sea.

"Hey—!" I forced out through chattering teeth, reaching a hand out toward her. "Wait!"

Before my fingers could even brush the frayed hem of her cloak, a sudden spark of violent blue light erupted around her collapsing form.

*FLASH!*

In the span of a single heartbeat, the girl dissolved into thin air—vanishing without a trace, leaving behind only an empty patch of snow and the faint imprint where her knees had rested.

A black crow, which had been swooping down from the sky toward her, pulled up sharply with a frantic, echoing scream.

*"KA! KA!"*

The bird landed heavily on a sharp outcropping of ice just a few feet away, tilting its head in sheer bewilderment, dark obsidian eyes scanning the empty snow where its companion had stood a second before.

I stared at the empty space in shock, but the biting Antarctic wind cut through my wet clothes like rusted knives, snapping me back to reality. Hypothermia was setting in rapidly. 

One thought roared louder than the blizzard in my mind.

*Azure. Liezt.*

"Azure!" I shouted, scrambling to my feet, my chest heaving as I looked around the chaotic cliff top. "Azure! Where are you?!"

"Brother Zeeran!" 

Through the swirling veil of white snow, a familiar figure pushed her way out of a deep drift. 

Azure stood upright, her sleek silver-blue hair plastered to her face with freezing seawater, her posture unyielding despite the absence of her arms. Without limbs to steady herself in the slippery snow, she used her specialized leg suit to balance perfectly against the fierce Antarctic wind.

I rushed toward her, my boots sinking deep into the snow, and caught her against my chest. She leaned into me, her cool cheek resting against my soaked coat as I wrapped my arms around her shivering frame.

"Are you injured?" I asked urgently, checking her head and neck for any signs of trauma from the ocean fall. "Did you sustain any structural damage during the warp?"

"Negative," Azure replied, her quiet, serene voice steady despite the freezing temperature. "My internal thermal regulators are operating at seventy-eight percent capacity. Physical integrity is nominal. Brother... the spatial distortion that brought us here dragged more than just organic life forms."

She turned her gaze toward the edge of the cliff, where a massive mound of snow and twisted steel debris lay half-buried.

My heart leaped into my throat. "Liezt's pod..."

"It is secured," Azure said quietly.

I didn't wait. Pushing through the deep snowdrift, I stumbled toward the edge of the cliff. 

There, jammed into a crevice between two high ice rocks, rested the massive two-point-four-meter Aegis crystal cube. The heavy cargo cables Azure had used to lock the pod inside Deck Two of the Ark were still wrapped tightly around its indestructible crystal frame, torn free from the ship's steel bulkhead during the violent spatial pull.

I dropped to my knees beside the cube, frantically wiping away the thick layer of frost and snow clinging to the crystal surface.

Inside the glowing, transparent lattice, suspended in clear cryo-stasis, was Liezt. Her eyes were closed in peaceful sleep, her small body completely untouched by the ocean water or the freezing Antarctic blizzard outside. The internal blue light of the Twilight Transmogrifier pulsed steadily, maintaining her life support without a single disruption.

A breath of overwhelming relief escaped my lips, turning to mist in the freezing air. 

"She's safe," I whispered, resting my forehead against the cold crystal boundary. "She's safe."

"Zeeran!" Erika's voice barked through the howling wind. 

She was striding through the crowd of shivering refugees, her uniform torn and drenched, but her posture authoritative as ever. Beside her, Belle was crouching near a group of terrified civilian Crows, wrapping them in whatever spare dry blankets had survived the transit, while Dr. Griffin attended to a coughing Raven soldier.

Erika stopped beside me, her eyes darting from Liezt's pod to the vast, endless white desert stretching behind us.

"We lost the Ark," Erika said, her voice hard as iron despite the tremble in her jaw. "The ship went down in the coastal trench. But fifty-two survivors made it to this cliff. What in God's name was that girl? Where did she go?"

I glanced back toward the empty patch of snow, where the lone black crow remained perched on the stone, watching us with quiet, eerie intelligence.

*"Ka,"* the bird rasped softly into the blizzard.

"I don't know who she was," I said slowly, standing up beside Azure and Erika as the blizzard howled around us. "But she brought us to Antarctica alive. We have no ship, no supplies, and no shelter. But we are here."

I turned my attention to the coordinates etched into my mind. Our primary Twilight research laboratory—the hidden sub-surface facility where Zyfel was stationed—lay roughly twelve kilometers inland, carved into the ancient granite bedrock beneath the glacial shelf. 

"Everyone, form up!" I called out, my voice cutting through the gale with calm, decisive authority as I took the lead. "We move inland immediately. The sub-zero exposure will kill us within an hour if we remain on this ridge. I know the exact coordinates to our underground lab—follow my pace!"

Azure stepped up to lead the heavy cargo transport, utilizing her specialized suit interface to lock a spatial tether around Liezt's crystal pod, dragging the massive Aegis cube smoothly across the snowdrifts. Belle and Dr. Griffin quickly rounded up the shivering Crows and Ravens into a tight, synchronized marching column, falling into step behind us.

As the survivors began marching off the high cliff ridge and into the blowing white mist, Erika stayed behind a moment longer.

She stood alone at the edge of the snowy outcrop, her heavy combat boots planted firmly in the frost, looking down at the small black crow perched on the jagged ice. 

The bird tilted its head, ruffling its obsidian feathers against the biting storm, letting out a small, forlorn rasp into the empty air—left completely stranded now that its master had vanished into thin air.

Erika's strict expression softened slightly beneath her frost-coated brow. She stepped closer to the icy rock and extended her heavy leather-gloved forearm into the freezing wind. 

"Hey," Erika spoke softly, her voice remarkably gentle against the roaring gale. "Your friend is gone... but you don't have to freeze out here by yourself."

Kaka stared at her with sharp, dark eyes for a long pause, shifting his weight on the icy stone. Then, with a low, agreeable *"Ka!"*, the crow hopped off the rock and landed squarely on Erika's leather glove, his sharp talons securing a firm grip. He fluttered up onto her shoulder, nestling against her high coat collar to shelter from the wind.

A faint, subtle smile touched Erika's lips as she glanced at her new little companion. Turning back toward the white horizon, she hurried through the snow drifts to catch up with the marching column—following Zeeran and the Ark team as they pressed forward into the frozen wilderness.

***

The whiteout struck within twenty minutes.

What had started as a biting coastal wind erupted into a ferocious Antarctic blizzard. The world dissolved into a blinding wall of roaring frost and swirling snow, reducing visibility to less than two meters. Temperatures plummeted drastically, biting through soaked coats and freezing moisture directly onto eyelashes and skin.

"Keep together!" Erika shouted against the roar of the tempest, her arm raised to shield her face. Kaka burrowed deeper under her high collar, his feathers puffed against the razor-sharp ice needles slicing through the air. "Don't fall behind! If you lose sight of the person ahead of you, you're dead!"

The survivors marched in a tight, desperate line. Dr. Griffin and Belle were moving along the flanks, helping those whose legs were buckling under the bitter cold. Azure led the middle of the group, effortlessly pulling Liezt’s heavy Aegis crystal pod through the deep snowdrifts with her mechanical suit's spatial tether, her internal thermal regulators humming as she monitored the group's vitals.

Erika trudged up to the front of the column, her boots sinking knee-deep into the powder with every step. She caught up to Zeeran, whose coat was already caked in a thick layer of rime.

"Zeeran!" she yelled above the screeching gale, teeth chattering violently. "How much further?! We can't survive in this environment much longer! There’s nothing out here—it's just flat white snow in every direction! Where is this underground facility of yours?!"

Zeeran kept his gaze fixed ahead, his eyes narrowed as his internal ocular overlay calculated coordinates against spatial magnetic readings.

"We are approaching the perimeter now," Zeeran replied, his voice calm despite the frost clinging to his lips. "The facility isn't underground in the conventional sense, Erika. Our primary Antarctic division is known as the **Ziggurat Tower**."

"Ziggurat Tower?" Erika blinked, wiping frost from her eyes as she glanced around the desolate, empty icy plain. "I don't see any tower! There's not even a mound out here!"

"You wouldn't see it even if we were standing right beside it," Zeeran explained. "The Ziggurat Tower is equipped with an advanced optical cloak—a passive light-refraction matrix developed by Twilight scientists. It takes advantage of the ambient sunlight reflecting off the vast, uninterrupted white snowfield. The structural surfaces split and bend light waves around the exterior, rendering the entire massive complex completely invisible to both the naked eye and standard optical scanners."

Erika looked around at the infinite expanse of blinding white snow with renewed astonishment, though her body shivered violently from the sub-zero chill. "An invisible tower... hidden right in the middle of a blizzard."

"Precisely," Zeeran nodded. "Which is why we need to locate the primary manual activation node."

After what felt like an agonizing eternity of fighting against the relentless blizzard, Zeeran suddenly came to a halt. The marching column behind them collapsed onto the snow, gasping and shivering, huddling together for warmth.

Zeeran scanned the Featureless white ground beneath his feet. "We are at the exact coordinates."

He dropped to his knees in the deep snowdrift, frantically sweeping his gloved hands through the heavy ice layers. He cleared a patch of snow, but found only solid blue glacier ice beneath.

"Where is it..." Zeeran muttered, his tone losing its usual calm composure.

He shifted three feet to the left, digging deeper into the frozen crust, his breathing turning rapid and uneven. He struck solid ice again.

"No... it should be right here. The magnetic benchmark was calibrated to this exact vector..." A surge of panic flickered in his eyes. His hands moved faster, violently scraping away snow and ice, his fingers numb inside his soaked gloves. If the coordinate shift had been altered by seismic shifts or spatial distortion from their teleportation, fifty people would freeze to death on this plain within minutes.

"Zeeran?" Erika asked, stepping forward, sensing his sudden distress. "Is something wrong?"

"It's here... it has to be here..." Zeeran breathed, his heart hammering against his ribs as he moved another meter to the north, throwing snow aside in desperate, frantic sweeps.

*CLANG!*

A sharp metallic ring echoed beneath his hands.

Zeeran froze. He rapidly cleared away the remaining layer of packed snow, revealing a heavy, metallic circular plate embedded deep within the bedrock. Etched into its center was the glowing silver crest of the Twilight Organization.

A heavy breath of relief escaped Zeeran's chest. "Found it."

He placed his palm firmly over the center of the metallic plate, pressing down on the hidden manual release mechanism to engage the primary circuit.

*HUMMMMMM—!*

The ground beneath them trembled violently. 

The snow beneath the entire expedition began to vibrate, and a deep, subterranean hum reverberated through the ice beneath their feet. Golden light erupted from the edges of the circular plate, tracing intricate glowing lines across the snowfield for hundreds of meters in every direction.

*WHIRRRRRR!*

High above them, the air itself began to warp and shimmer. The white snow-reflected light that had rendered the structure invisible began to distort, peeling back like a dissolving curtain of liquid glass.

As the optical cloak deactivated, the howling blizzard seemed to part around a colossal silhouette rising into the sky.

The survivors gasped in awe, dropping their hands as they stared upward.

Emerging from the blinding snowstorm was a majestic, gargantuan step-pyramid—a massive **Ziggurat** stretching hundreds of meters into the Antarctic sky. But it was not built of stone or steel. The entire gigantic structure was forged from pure, polished, radiant gold, gleaming with an unearthly brilliance that illuminated the dark, stormy skies of the frozen continent.

Intricate mechanical patterns and glowing blue light conduits pulsed along every golden step of the ancient, towering facility, casting a warm, protective aura over the frozen waste below.

Zeeran stood up, wiping the frost from his face as he looked up at the towering golden structure.

"Welcome," Zeeran said quietly, turning back to Erika and the weary survivors of the Ark. "To the Ziggurat Tower."
