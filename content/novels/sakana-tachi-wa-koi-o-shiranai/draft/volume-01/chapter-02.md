The water inside the zinc bucket was murky, smelling of deep-shelf kelp, crushed cockle shells, and the raw, cold salt of a moon tide.

Umika Minagi took another step across the damp floor of Preservation Lab 3. Her pale, bare feet made soft, wet slapping sounds against the concrete, leaving faint prints of sea-mist in the dust. Her loose white sailor blouse swayed, unbuttoned at the top two eyelets, and the oversized fisherman's windbreaker hung off her slender shoulders like a shed pupal skin.

"Kaidō-kun," Chihaya whispered. The pen in her hand trembled with enough force that ink spat onto the edge of her pristine abstract paper. "Who... what... why is there an barefoot urchin standing in my certified bio-hazard facility carrying industrial wastewater?!"

"She isn't an urchin," I said, rising from my stool. "Urchins belong to the class *Echinoidea*, characterized by radial symmetry and calcareous test spines. This is a human female. Specifically, the transfer student."

"I don't care about her taxonomic classification!" Chihaya shrieked, slamming both hands onto the stainless-steel island. "This laboratory contains three hundred liters of high-grade formaldehyde, rare abyssal samples from the Ryūgū trench, and an electron microscope subsidized by the Kujirazaki Oceanarium Foundation! She isn't wearing rubberized safety booties! Her epidermal layer is actively dripping ambient moisture onto my linoleum!"

Umika didn't even blink. 

She bypassed Chihaya entirely, sliding past the stainless-steel table with an effortless, serpentine grace that didn't disturb a single glass slide. She halted six inches in front of me. 

Up close, the chill radiating from her skin was unmistakable—a damp, subterranean cold that felt like opening the hatch of an ice-trawler's hold. Her hair had dried slightly in the drafty corridor, shimmering with faint, translucent swirls of silver and turquoise, like the nacre on the interior of an abalone shell.

Slowly, without a word, she raised her left hand.

Her slender, pale index finger reached out and touched my right earlobe.

Her skin was shocking: not merely cool, but smooth as a polished river stone, lacking the microscopic friction of dry epithelial tissue.

"Hey!" Chihaya squawked, her voice pitching up an entire octave. "What do you think you're touching?!"

Umika ignored her. Her head tilted slightly to the right, her eyes—iridescent, deep-sea navy flecked with luminous specks like plankton under a lantern—stared into mine with utter, unnerving focus.

"Warm," she murmured. Her voice had the soft, muffled timbre of sound heard underwater through a diving helmet. "Surface blood is very noisy. Thump-thump. Like a piston."

"Your peripheral capillary temperature is still depressed," I observed, standing completely still as her finger traced the curve of my ear. "Based on dermal contact, your epidermis is hovering at approximately 33.8 degrees Celsius. Walking across bare concrete without rubber indoor slippers drastically increases conductive heat loss through the plantar fascia. Why did you discard your footwear?"

Umika looked down at her bare feet, wiggling her toes against the cold floor.

"Slippers are bad," she said flatly. "They pinch like clam valves. The stone wants to speak with the skin. Why do surface humans wrap leather around their fins?"

"Those are feet, not fins," I corrected. "And leather provides traction against crushed barnacle shells and diesel slicks. Furthermore, what is in the bucket?"

At the mention of the bucket, Umika’s expression didn't change, but her shoulders relaxed by a millimeter. She lowered the battered zinc pail onto the stainless-steel dissection table, right next to Chihaya's mass-spectrometry monitor.

*CLANG.*

"Do not place harbor refuse on my sterile workstation!" Chihaya gasped, lunging forward with a bottle of laboratory disinfectant.

"Wait, Senpai," I said, holding up a hand. "Look at the surface tension."

Chihaya paused, her spray nozzle hovering mid-air.

Inside the zinc pail, swirling through three liters of dark seawater, was a creature the size of a tea saucer. At first glance, it resembled a common hermit crab tucked inside a cracked turban shell. But as it shifted against the metal bottom, its legs emerged—long, translucent, and jointed with delicate, glass-like carapaces that glowed with a rhythmic, pulsating sapphire luminescence.

Chihaya’s jaw dropped. Her red-framed glasses slid down her nose. "That's... that's a *Bathynomus* symbiont? No, look at the antennae structure. That's an abyssal ghost-hermit! They only inhabit depths below eight hundred meters along the volcanic vents of the Ryūgū trench! How could a live specimen survive in an unpressurized bucket?!"

"I found him by the tide-glass rocks," Umika said quietly, peering into the water. "He was lonely. The tide went down, but he forgot how to swim backward. So I brought him."

"You... you 'brought him'?!" Chihaya looked ready to tear her hair out. "You carried an uncataloged abyssal decapod through the school corridors like a stray goldfish?!"

"The water has elevated mineral salinity," I noted, dipping a pinky into the bucket and tasting it. "It's near-shore benthic upwelling water. The deep cold currents are rising toward the coast."

Before Chihaya could resume her scientific meltdown, the heavy wooden door of the lab swung open with a resounding bang that rattled the specimen jars on the top shelf.

"Minagi-san!"

Standing in the doorway, breathing hard and clutching her aluminum clipboard like a shield, was the Student Council President.

Shiori Ryūgū looked slightly disheveled—an unprecedented sight for the hereditary maiden of the Dragon Palace. A few stray strands of obsidian hair had slipped loose from her tortoiseshell combs, clinging to her damp brow. Her collar was slightly askew, and she still held an emergency brass whistle in her left hand.

"There you are!" Shiori gasped, marching into the lab with furious, purposeful strides. "I turned my back for three minutes to file your transfer ledger at the registrar's office, and you vanish into the basement wing! Do you have any idea how many disciplinary regulations you violated walking through the second-year hall without an escort?!"

"President," I nodded. "Your vestibular balance appears significantly restored. The ginger stabilized your inner-ear fluid, I presume?"

Shiori flinched. A violent blush washed over her pale cheeks, and she instinctively cleared her throat, adjusting her blazer lapels with exaggerated dignity.

"That... that is completely irrelevant to current administrative matters, Kaidō-san!" she snapped, refusing to meet my eyes. "And I did not ingest your barbaric harbor concoction! I merely... tested a microscopic fragment for toxicological compliance!"

"You had ginger fibers stuck between your incisors this morning," I pointed out.

"Silence!" she hissed, her face burning crimson. She spun around to face Umika, holding out an official white folder stamped with the vermilion three-wave crest of the Ryūgū Shrine. "Minagi-san. Your enrollment documentation is finalized. As your legal municipal sponsor and shrine guardian, I have completed your preliminary matriculation. However, you were explicitly instructed to wait in the Student Council reception annex!"

Chihaya folded her arms, stepping forward with narrowed eyes behind her glasses.

"Hold on just a moment, Ryūgū-san," Chihaya said, her voice dripping with venomous polite condescension. "Did I hear you correctly? *You* sponsored this barefoot drifter? And by what authority did she wander into the Marine Specimen Laboratory?"

Shiori turned, meeting Chihaya’s glare with icy composure.

"By the authority of the Kaiyō High School Club Charter, Section 8," Shiori replied smoothly, tapping the folder. "As of two o'clock this afternoon, Umika Minagi is officially enrolled as a special-admissions transfer student under the cultural custody of the Ryūgū Shrine. And per her specific admissions directive, she requires placement in an academic extracurricular."

Chihaya’s eyebrows shot up. "And you dumped her in *my* club?!"

"Your club," Shiori said with a tight, predatory smile, "had precisely two members: you and Kaidō-san. Under Article 12 of the prefectural budget code, any extracurricular organization with fewer than three active members faces immediate dissolution and total reclamation of its allocated research funds by the Student Council."

Chihaya froze. The color drained from her face. "You... you wouldn't dare."

"I would," Shiori replied serenely. "In fact, I had the dissolution decree drafted on my desk this morning. However, since the shrine requires Minagi-san to remain in an environment equipped with high-salinity seawater facilities and biological monitoring, I graciously assigned her here. You now have three members. Your club is saved. You may thank me by keeping her within this room."

"I don't want your charity, you tyrannical shrine maiden!" Chihaya fumed. "This is an elite taxonomic research institute, not a daycare for seaside castaways!"

"She is not a castaway," Shiori countered, her eyes darkening with ancient, sacred gravity. "She is a *Yoriko*. The shrine recognizes her as a sacred ward of the sea."

"Superstitious nonsense! In the modern Meiji-era academy, we operate on empirical oceanography, not shrine fables!"

While the two of them squared off like a pair of territorial reef morays, their foreheads practically touching across the stainless-steel counter, Umika quietly drifted closer to me.

She reached out, took the sleeve of my navy blazer in her cool fingers, and gently tugged.

"Tsubasa," she whispered.

"Yes, Minagi-san?"

"They are very loud," she noted in her calm, flat voice. "Like gulls fighting over fish guts on the pier. Are all land-females this noisy?"

"Generally, territorial displays in social primates involve elevated vocal volume and posturing to establish dominance," I explained. "It consumes considerable caloric energy with minimal survival utility, but human social hierarchies depend heavily on such rituals."

Umika nodded slowly, accepting this completely. Then, with total disregard for personal boundaries, she leaned her head against my shoulder, closing her eyes with a quiet sigh of relief.

"Your scent is good," she murmured, her cold breath brushing against my neck. "Deep water and salt-ice. Like the trench before sunrise."

"That is skipjack blood and industrial anti-rust lubricant," I corrected. "Though the skipjack was remarkably fresh."

*KICK.*

The frosted-glass door was kicked open with the concussive force of a breaching swordfish.

"WING-HEAD!" 

Matsuri Isaki stood in the threshold, panting, a heavy plastic crate of spooling fluorocarbon line resting against her hip, a pair of heavy wire-cutters tucked into the pocket of her rolled-up uniform skirt.

"I went all the way down to the skiff dock, and my dad said you never picked up the heavy-gauge tuna leaders, and if we don't mend the outer drift nets before the night tide—"

Matsuri stopped dead.

Her eyes swept the room.

First, she saw Chihaya Kujirazaki, disheveled in her white lab coat, trembling with academic rage.

Second, she saw Shiori Ryūgū, the pristine Student Council President, red-faced and clutching shrine paperwork.

Third, and most fatally, her gaze locked onto the leather couch in the center of the lab.

There sat Tsubasa Kaidō. 

And resting her head comfortably against his shoulder, clutching his blazer sleeve with both hands like an affectionate otter, was an impossibly ethereal girl with silver-blue hair, bare feet, and an unbuttoned uniform blouse.

Matsuri's crate of heavy fishing line slipped from her fingers.

*CRASH.*

Dozens of plastic spools and lead sinkers scattered across the concrete floor, rolling into every corner of the room.

"Wha..." Matsuri's jaw worked soundlessly. The golden tan of her cheeks was instantly obliterated by a tidal wave of scarlet that engulfed her face all the way to the roots of her chestnut ponytail. "What... what... *WHAT IS GOING ON HERE?!*"

Chihaya spun around. "Isaki! Watch your step! Those lead sinkers are rolling toward my centrifuge!"

"I don't give a damn about your centrifuge!" Matsuri screamed, storming into the room and pointing a trembling, calloused forefinger directly at Umika. "Who is that?! Why is she half-naked?! And why is she clinging to Tsubasa like a remora on a tiger shark?!"

Umika opened one eye, still resting against my shoulder. She blinked at Matsuri's flushed face.

"Remoras," Umika said softly, "have specialized suction discs on their dorsal fins. I do not have a suction disc. I am using my fingers."

To demonstrate, she tightened her grip on my sleeve.

"A-A-Aaaaagh!" Matsuri shrieked, clutching her own head with both hands. "Tsubasa! Push her off! Why are you just sitting there like a dead flounder?!"

"She is suffering from mild hypothermic inertia," I answered calmly. "Conductive body heat transfer is the standard first-aid protocol for marine drifters. If I push her away, her peripheral circulation will suffer further vasoconstriction."

"I'll give you vasoconstriction, you idiot!" Matsuri lunged forward, grabbing my left arm and yanking with all the strength of someone who spent her childhood hauling fifty-kilo longlines out of the surf. "Get up! We have nets to fix!"

"Let go of him, Isaki-san!" Shiori commanded, stepping between them with her clipboard raised like a barrier. "Kaidō-san is currently engaged in official Student Council business regarding the custody and orientation of a newly registered transfer student! Harbor maintenance is an unsanctioned extracurricular activity!"

"And who gave you the right to run this clubroom, Ryūgū?!" Chihaya roared, stepping in from the other side. "This is *my* laboratory! All of you get out! The drifter, the dock-rat, the shrine maiden, and the mackerel-brain! Out! *Out!*"

"Stop pulling," I said, feeling my blazer seams groan as Matsuri pulled left, Chihaya grabbed my collar to push me toward the door, and Umika anchored herself tighter to my right arm. "The tensile strength of standard Kaiyō academy wool is only forty-five kilograms per square inch. At this rate, the lateral torque will cause structural failure of the shoulder seam."

"Shut up!" all three of them yelled in unison.

*BZZZZZZZZT.*

A sharp, electric snap cut through the chaos.

Every head in the room froze.

In the corner of the laboratory, beneath the high, reinforced glass windows that looked out into the subterranean harbor flume, the darkness of the seawater had changed.

It wasn't black anymore. 

A deep, phosphorescent crimson—the color of an open wound glowing under ultraviolet light—was pulsing through the submerged glass tunnel. 

At the same instant, inside the zinc bucket on the dissection island, the abyssal ghost-hermit crab began scrabbling frantically against the metal walls. Its blue bioluminescent legs flickered, shifting rapidly from sapphire to a warning flash of harsh, jagged violet.

Umika went completely rigid.

She released my sleeve. Slowly, like a sleepwalker pulled by an invisible tide, she rose from the couch. Her bare feet carried her across the scattered lead sinkers toward the underwater observation glass.

"Umika?" Shiori called out, her voice suddenly dropping all its haughty defense, replaced by genuine shrine-maiden apprehension.

Umika placed both pale palms flat against the cold, thick acrylic of the harbor flume.

Beyond the glass, drifting through the dark waters of the bay twenty feet below the surface, was a long, ribbon-like silhouette. It was over seven meters long, shimmering with brilliant silver scales that caught the dim red light, crowned with an immense, feather-like crimson dorsal crest that undulated like silk ribbons in a gale.

"Ryūgū no Tsukai," I whispered, stepping up behind her.

"The Messenger of the Dragon Palace," Chihaya breathed, her academic rage instantly evaporating into scientific awe. "A giant oarfish... alive? In the shallow harbor waters?"

"No," Shiori said, her face pale as a ghost, her hand instinctively reaching up to clutch the sacred sea-glass amulet at her throat. Her knuckles trembled. "They never come into the shallows unless the trench is breaking. The deep currents... they're rising."

Umika pressed her forehead against the glass, her reflection blending with the glowing scarlet water outside.

"The sea is waking up," she whispered. Her voice hummed with a low, resonant frequency that made the brass tide-bells high above the school begin to chime in the distance. "The deep water... is looking for its home."
