The **Celestial Tides (*Tenkai-ryū* / 天海流)** are permanent moisture-dense atmospheric rivers that circulate over the Shiozaki coastline. First chronicled in Heian-era coastal shrine scrolls, the phenomenon is the physical embodiment of the legendary boundary between the surface world, the deep ocean, and the celestial realm (*Tokoyo-no-Kuni*).

## Nature of the Current
Unlike normal weather patterns, the Celestial Tides possess:
- **Buoyant Vapor Density:** Sea-clouds carry high concentrations of suspended oceanic minerals and spiritual moisture (*kamishio*), allowing pelagic animals to swim through air currents without losing buoyancy or suffocating.
- **Tidal Schedules:** The altitude and speed of the aerial currents follow lunar phases. During **Spring High Tides (*Ōshio*)**, the currents drop low enough to touch classroom windows and temple steeples. During **Neap Tides (*Koshio*)**, the marine currents retreat into high stratosphere cirrus clouds.
- **Bioluminescent Phosphorescence:** On summer evenings, the sky glows with emerald and sapphire hues (*shiranui*), illuminating the bellies of overhead humpbacks drifting against the stars.

## Folklore & Cultural Significance
According to Shinto traditions of the Ryūgū Shrine:
- Sky-whales are considered emissaries of **Otohime**, the guardian sovereign of the Sea Palace.
- It is considered severe spiritual sacrilege to intentionally harm an aerial creature while it traverses the clouds over land.
- Harbors hold the annual **Tide-Welcoming Festival (*Shio-mukae Matsuri*)**, where locals cast lanterns into both the harbor waters and the sky using helium-lift kelp paper to pray for romantic fortune and abundant harvests.
